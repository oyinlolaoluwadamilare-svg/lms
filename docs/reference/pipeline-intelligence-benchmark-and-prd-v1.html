Pipeline Intelligence — Benchmark Assessment & Product Requirements Document

Prepared by: Sales Analyst review (business development capability assessment) Date: 25 July 2026 Version: 1.0 Product under review: Pipeline Intelligence — Workforce Leads & Pipeline Management platform (Workforce Group tenant, Enterprise plan) Audience: Product owner, engineering team, Sales Operations, Practice Directors

0. How to read this document

The document is deliberately organised across three dimensions, because the product is already live and partially adopted:

Dimension 1 — What already works. A verified inventory of current capability, including who can use each feature. This protects the team from rebuilding what exists.

Dimension 2 — What a world-class product must have. A benchmark of non-negotiable capability for a world-class business development organisation, each mapped to the role who uses it, the role accountable for the data, and the internal function that builds it.

Dimension 3 — The gap. The delta between the two, severity-rated, with evidence.

Section 5 onward is the Product Requirements Document proper: objectives, personas, functional requirements with acceptance criteria, data model, non-functional requirements, phasing and risks.

Benchmark basis. Capability categories were benchmarked against the published product taxonomies and standard feature sets of activity-based-selling and pipeline CRM platforms — Salesforce Sales Cloud, HubSpot Sales Hub, Pipedrive and Zoho CRM. The recurring capability domains across all four are: automation, CRM core records, customisation, email and communications, insights and reporting, integrations, leads and prospecting, processes/pipeline/activities, permissions and visibility, and increasingly AI assistance. These domains form the spine of Dimension 2.

Inspection method. All navigation surfaces were inspected across five simulated roles (Tenant Admin, Executive read-only, Director, Team Lead, BDM): deal capture modal, deal detail page, board and table pipeline views, advanced filters, Business Lines workspace, Dashboard, Analytics, Files, Notifications, Settings, and the Admin panel (pipeline stages, practice lines, roles and permissions, data backup). Absent modules were confirmed by direct route probing.

1. Executive Summary

Pipeline Intelligence is considerably more mature than a prototype. It has genuine multi-tenancy, a five-tier role hierarchy scoped by practice line, a configurable stage engine with probability thresholds, a custom-field builder, seat-managed team administration, and a backup and export regime. At the level of recording and reporting the pipeline, it is competitive with mid-market CRM.

The weakness is not the deal data model. It is the execution layer around the deal. A world-class business development organisation runs on activities, ownership and next actions. This product currently runs on records and stage labels. Three findings dominate.

Finding 1 — Defect, not a gap (P0). An engagement logger already exists. The deal page exposes a "Log Activity" modal capturing type (note, call, email, meeting, follow-up), an activity date with explicit support for backdating so that entries stay in the correct stage, a summary, and an outcome. It is not exposed to the BDM role — the very role that performs the engagement. A BDM sees only an "Internal Notes — Read-only snapshot" panel. The capability is roughly 70% built and mis-permissioned rather than missing. This single fix unlocks most of the value the organisation is currently missing.

Finding 2 — Gap (P0). There is no task object anywhere in the system. The tasks route returns a 404. The "Outcome — optional next step or result" field is free text with no assignee, due date, status or reminder. Consequently nothing can be delegated, nothing can become overdue, and no one can be chased. Notifications carry only system events (stage changes, backup alerts), never human commitments.

Finding 3 — Gap (P1). Stage movement is persisted as date fields on the deal record, not as events. The Analytics page openly degrades as a result: the Stage Conversion Funnel states that current-stage distribution is shown because historical stage movement is unavailable. Conversion rate, sales velocity and cycle-time metrics are therefore structurally unreliable, even though the days-in-stage filter and the stage-age legend (7 / 21 / 45 / 46+ days) work well.

Both hypotheses raised by the product owner — continuously updated, date-tagged engagement notes persisting until the deal closes, and the ability to assign tasks to others — are confirmed, correctly prioritised, and are the two highest-value items in the backlog. To them this assessment adds a second tier: contacts and buying-committee management, activation of the already-scaffolded email connectors, structured win/loss reason capture, and stage-event history.

Data hygiene symptoms of the missing execution layer are already visible in live records: an active deal carrying "Deal Author: Unknown Author", "Primary Owner: Unassigned" and "Close Date: Not scheduled", while still contributing a weighted forecast figure to the practice roll-up.

Part B — Product Requirements Document

5. Purpose, Objectives and Success Metrics

5.1 Problem statement

Pipeline Intelligence answers the question "what is in the pipeline?" well. It cannot answer "what is being done about it, by whom, and by when?" Because engagement is not captured and commitments cannot be assigned, the organisation cannot manage activity, cannot coach on inputs, and cannot trust its own conversion metrics. Every downstream analytic weakness in this document traces back to that single missing layer.

5.2 Objectives

O1 — Every active deal carries a verifiable engagement history and a scheduled next action. No deal should be able to sit untouched and undetected until close.

O2 — Every commitment is owned by a named person with a date. Work must be delegable, visible and chaseable inside the product rather than in side-channel chat and email.

O3 — Every pipeline metric is derived from recorded events rather than manually maintained fields. Analytics must stop degrading and start being defensible in a board meeting.

O4 — Relationships are held by the organisation, not by the individual. Client, stakeholder and engagement knowledge must survive staff turnover.

6. Functional Requirements

Requirement identifiers are stable references for the backlog. Each requirement carries a user story, acceptance criteria, and any data implication.

EPIC 1 — Engagement Timeline (resolves D1, D2 · Priority P0)

FR-1.1 — Expose activity logging to all writing roles As a BDM, I need to log an engagement on my own deal, because I am the person who had the conversation. Acceptance criteria: the Log Activity action renders for BDM, Team Lead, Director and Tenant Admin on any deal within the user's permission scope; the Executive role continues to see no write action; the existing modal is reused without redesign; a BDM may log against any deal they own, co-own or authored. This should be treated as a permission-matrix defect and shipped ahead of the rest of the epic if the release train allows.

FR-1.2 — Append-only engagement timeline on the deal record As a Director, I need to read the full engagement history in date order, because that is how I judge deal health. Acceptance criteria: the deal page renders all activities newest first, each showing type icon, engagement date, author name, summary and outcome, with the record creation timestamp available on hover or expansion; entries persist for the life of the deal and remain fully visible after Closed Won or Closed Lost; the timeline merges activity entries and stage-change events into a single chronological stream; the current "Read-only snapshot" panel is retired; any legacy Internal Strategy Notes content is migrated into a note-type activity dated to the deal creation date and attributed to the deal author.

FR-1.3 — Engagement date integrity As a Sales Operations lead, I need the engagement date to reflect when the engagement actually happened, because backdated entries must not distort stage timing. Acceptance criteria: activity date is mandatory and defaults to today; future dates are rejected with an inline validation message directing the user to create a task instead; backdating is permitted without limit but any entry backdated more than 14 days is visually marked as late-logged; the creation timestamp is always stored separately from the engagement date and both are retained; all dates render in the user's configured timezone and date format.

FR-1.4 — Edit and delete controls with an audit trail As a Tenant Admin, I need engagement history to be tamper-evident, because it is the evidential basis for performance management. Acceptance criteria: an author may edit their own entry within 24 hours of creation, after which the entry becomes read-only; every edit stores a prior-value revision record with actor and timestamp, and the entry displays an "edited" marker with revision history on demand; hard deletion is not available to any role; Directors and Tenant Admins may mark an entry as retracted with a mandatory reason, which strikes it through in the timeline without removing it; all edit, retract and create events write to the audit log.

FR-1.5 — Activity type taxonomy and outcome disposition As a Team Lead, I need to distinguish a client meeting from an internal note, because they carry very different weight. Acceptance criteria: the existing five types are retained (note, call, email, meeting, follow-up) and extended with site visit, proposal walkthrough and internal review; each type is flagged as either client-facing or internal, and only client-facing types update the last-engaged field; the outcome field gains an optional structured disposition (positive, neutral, negative, no response) alongside its free text, feeding sentiment trend reporting.

FR-1.6 — Last engaged derived field and staleness banding As a Director, I need to see at a glance which deals have gone quiet, because silence is the leading indicator of a loss. Acceptance criteria: the deal stores a last_engaged_at value derived from the most recent client-facing activity date; the value appears on the deal header, board card, table column (sortable) and in advanced filters as a "days since last engagement" numeric filter; staleness bands reuse the existing colour legend thresholds of 7, 21 and 45 days for visual consistency; deals with no engagement in over 30 days are automatically included in the Dashboard at-risk tile, which today counts only unread notifications and backup alerts; the derivation runs on write, not on a nightly batch, so the value is never stale itself.

FR-1.7 — Attachments on activity entries As a BDM, I need to attach the minutes or a signed note to the engagement record, because the artefact and the narrative belong together. Acceptance criteria: an activity accepts up to five file attachments within the tenant file-size policy; attachments inherit the deal's practice-line visibility; they also appear in the deal's document list with the activity as their provenance.

EPIC 2 — Tasks, Assignment and Delegation (resolves D3, D4, D24 · Priority P0)

FR-2.1 — Task object As a BDM, I need to record a commitment with a date, because an intention without a date is not a commitment. Acceptance criteria: a task carries title (mandatory), description, assignee (mandatory), assigner (system-set), due date (mandatory) with optional time, priority (low, normal, high, urgent), status (open, in progress, blocked, done, cancelled), an optional link to a deal, contact or originating activity, and a completion timestamp with completing actor; tasks may exist standalone or attached to a deal; completing a task optionally prompts the user to log a corresponding activity, and blocking a task requires a reason.

FR-2.2 — Assignment to other users As a Team Lead, I need to assign follow-up work to a colleague, because deals are worked by teams and not by individuals. Acceptance criteria: the assignee picker lists users within the actor's permission scope — a BDM may assign to co-owners of the deal, their Team Lead and their practice-line peers, a Team Lead to anyone in the practice, a Director to anyone in the practice, and a Tenant Admin to anyone in the tenant; assignment triggers an in-app notification and, where enabled, an email to the assignee; reassignment preserves the full assignment history on the task; the Executive role cannot assign.

FR-2.3 — Watchers, comments and @mentions As a Director, I need to comment on a task and pull a colleague in, because coordination should not leave the platform. Acceptance criteria: any user with visibility may add themselves or others as watchers; tasks support a threaded comment log with author and timestamp; typing @ surfaces an in-scope user picker, and mentioning a user notifies them and adds them as a watcher; @mentions are supported in task comments, activity summaries and Business Director Comments.

FR-2.4 — My Work view As a BDM, I need one screen that tells me what to do today, because I should not have to open fourteen deals to find out. Acceptance criteria: a new primary navigation item presents tasks grouped as overdue, due today, due this week and later, with the deal, client and priority visible on each row; inline actions allow complete, snooze (with a new date and a mandatory reason if snoozed more than twice), reassign and open deal; a companion tab shows tasks the user has assigned to others with their status, so delegation can be tracked; Team Leads and Directors gain a team view filterable by assignee, and a leaderboard of open, overdue and completed counts by person.

FR-2.5 — Next-action enforcement on every open deal As a Director, I need every live deal to have a next step, because a deal without a next step is not being worked. Acceptance criteria: the deal record exposes a next_action derived from the earliest open task with a due date; the deal header displays the next action and its due date, or a prominent "No next step" warning; a "No next step" filter and a Dashboard tile list all open deals lacking an open task; advancing a deal's stage prompts, but does not force, creation of the next task; marking a deal Closed Won or Closed Lost cancels its remaining open tasks after confirmation.

FR-2.6 — Reminders, overdue handling and SLA escalation As a Team Lead, I need the system to chase, because chasing manually does not scale. Acceptance criteria: reminders fire at a configurable offset before due date and again on the due date; on becoming overdue, the assignee is notified immediately and the assigner at 24 hours; tasks overdue beyond a tenant-configurable threshold, defaulting to 72 hours, escalate to the Team Lead and then the Director, reusing the existing escalation and notification infrastructure; escalation events appear in the existing escalated-deals panel on the Business Lines page; all reminder behaviour respects the user's timezone and honours their notification preferences.

FR-2.7 — Task templates by stage As a Sales Operations lead, I need standard playbook steps created automatically, because consistency is what makes conversion predictable. Acceptance criteria: a Tenant Admin may define template task sets per stage per practice line, each with a title, default assignee rule (deal owner, deal author, or a named role) and a due-date offset; entering a stage creates the template tasks; users may decline the set at the point of creation; templates are managed within the existing Admin panel structure.

EPIC 3 — Accounts and Contacts (resolves D6 · Priority P1)

FR-3.1 — Account object. Acceptance criteria: client organisations become first-class records carrying name, industry, region, size band, parent account, website and owner; existing free-text client organisation values are migrated with a de-duplication pass and an admin review queue for ambiguous matches; every deal links to exactly one account; account names are unique per tenant with fuzzy-match warning on creation.

FR-3.2 — Account 360 page. Acceptance criteria: a single page shows the account profile, all deals across all practice lines with stage and value, aggregate won revenue and open pipeline, all contacts, the merged engagement timeline across the account's deals, and all documents; visibility respects practice-line scoping, so a BDM sees only the deals they are entitled to see while still seeing that other practice-line relationships exist, without their values.

FR-3.3 — Contacts with buying-committee roles. Acceptance criteria: contacts carry first and last name, job title, email, phone, LinkedIn URL, account link, and a decision role from champion, economic buyer, influencer, technical evaluator, procurement, blocker or unknown; a deal may associate many contacts, exactly one flagged primary; the existing single contact-person field migrates to the primary contact; activities may be attributed to one or more contacts; a warning appears on any deal past Discovery Call with fewer than two associated contacts, as a single-threading risk indicator.

FR-3.4 — Contact engagement history. Acceptance criteria: each contact page lists every activity attributed to them across all deals, with a last-engaged value at contact level; contacts not engaged in 90 days on an open deal are flagged.

FR-3.5 — Relationship continuity on reassignment. Acceptance criteria: reassigning a deal or account owner never deletes or hides prior activity or contact records; a handover summary panel presents the last ten engagements, all open tasks and all contacts to the incoming owner.

EPIC 4 — Stage Event History and Analytics Integrity (resolves D5, D25 · Priority P1)

FR-4.1 — Stage-change event log. Acceptance criteria: every stage transition writes an immutable event recording deal, from-stage, to-stage, actor, timestamp and computed duration in the previous stage; events are written by the system on any transition path including edit-deal, board drag, and the Mark as Won and Mark as Lost actions; existing stage date fields are backfilled into synthetic events at migration, flagged as reconstructed so analytics can distinguish them; the stage-configuration warning already present in the Admin panel is extended to explain that renaming a stage preserves history under a stable stage identifier.

FR-4.2 — Rebuild the conversion funnel on event data. Acceptance criteria: the Analytics funnel reports true stage-to-stage conversion for deals entering each stage in the selected period; the current degradation message is removed; mean and median time in stage are shown per stage; the slowest stage by median is highlighted as the bottleneck; reconstructed events are excluded from cycle-time calculations and the exclusion is disclosed in the panel footnote.

FR-4.3 — Corrected sales velocity. Acceptance criteria: sales velocity is computed from event-derived cycle time rather than from the current-state approximation, using the standard formulation of qualified opportunities multiplied by average deal value multiplied by win rate divided by average cycle length; the calculation method is documented in an information tooltip.

FR-4.4 — Activity and performance analytics by person and team. Acceptance criteria: new Analytics panels report activity volume by type by user and by team, engagement coverage as the percentage of owned deals engaged in the period, task completion rate and on-time rate, and average time to first engagement after deal creation; all panels respect role scoping so a BDM sees only their own figures, a Team Lead the team, a Director the practice, and Executive and Tenant Admin the tenant.

FR-4.5 — Stage regression visibility. Acceptance criteria: deals moving backwards through stages are identified and reported, since backwards movement is a strong loss predictor and is currently invisible.

EPIC 5 — Email and Calendar Synchronisation (resolves D7 · Priority P1)

FR-5.1 — Activate the existing OAuth connectors. Acceptance criteria: Zoho, Google and Outlook connectors complete a real OAuth authorisation flow initiated by the individual user, never by an administrator on their behalf; connection state, connected account address and last sync time are displayed; disconnection revokes tokens and is available at any time; tokens are stored encrypted and are never exposed to the client.

FR-5.2 — Inbound and outbound email logging. Acceptance criteria: mail exchanged with an email address matching a known contact is automatically logged as an email-type activity against that contact and their open deals; matching is by exact address with a manual "link to deal" fallback for unmatched mail; users may mark individual threads private and exclude them; only subject, participants, timestamp and an optional body excerpt are stored, with body storage configurable off at tenant level for privacy; automatic logging updates last_engaged_at exactly as manual logging does.

FR-5.3 — Calendar synchronisation. Acceptance criteria: calendar events with an external participant matching a known contact create meeting-type activities on acceptance, dated to the event, with attendee list and duration; cancellations and reschedules update the corresponding activity; internal-only meetings are excluded by default.

FR-5.4 — Send from within the deal. Acceptance criteria: where a connector is active, a user may compose and send an email to a deal contact from the deal page, with the outbound message logged automatically; templates from Epic 10 are selectable at compose time.

EPIC 6 — Forecasting, Quotas and Win/Loss Discipline (resolves D8, D9, D11 · Priority P1)

FR-6.1 — Mandatory win and loss reasons at close. Acceptance criteria: Mark as Won requires a win reason from a configurable picklist, a final negotiated value and an actual close date; Mark as Lost requires a loss reason from a configurable picklist covering price, timing, no decision, lost to competitor, no budget, internal capability, unresponsive and other, plus mandatory free-text detail, plus a competitor name where the reason is lost to competitor; closing is blocked until these are supplied; reasons are configurable per tenant in the Admin panel; a loss-reason report is added to Analytics with breakdown by practice, value band and competitor.

FR-6.2 — Forecast categories independent of stage. Acceptance criteria: each open deal carries a forecast category of pipeline, best case, commit or closed, defaulting from stage probability but overridable by the deal owner with a reason; Directors may override the owner's category, with both values retained and the override visible; the weighted forecast panel is extended to show category totals alongside the existing probability-weighted figure.

FR-6.3 — Quotas and targets. Acceptance criteria: a Director or Tenant Admin may set a revenue target per user, per team and per practice line, per fiscal period; attainment is computed from closed-won value in the period and rolls up from user to team to practice to tenant; dashboards display attainment percentage, gap to target and pace against elapsed period; the current practice-level target reference in Analytics is superseded by this model.

FR-6.4 — Forecast accuracy tracking. Acceptance criteria: the commit forecast is snapshotted at the start of each period and at each subsequent week; at period close, committed versus actual is reported by user, team and practice, with variance percentage and a rolling four-period accuracy trend.

FR-6.5 — Mandatory ownership and close date on active deals. Acceptance criteria: a deal may not be saved in an active state without a primary owner and a scheduled close date; existing records that violate this rule appear in an admin remediation queue with bulk-assign capability; the "Unknown Author" state is eliminated by backfilling the creating user, and where unrecoverable, by an explicit "System import" author value.

EPIC 7 — Leads and Qualification (resolves D12, D13, D22 partially · Priority P2)

FR-7.1 — Lead object separate from deal. Acceptance criteria: leads carry contact and company details, source, practice line, owner, status of new, working, nurturing, qualified or disqualified, and a disqualification reason; leads convert to a deal with full preservation of activity history and contact records; conversion is one-way and audited; the pipeline board and value calculations exclude unconverted leads so that pipeline value is not inflated.

FR-7.2 — Qualification framework. Acceptance criteria: a configurable qualification block, defaulting to BANT with an optional MEDDIC extension, is available on leads and deals; a completeness percentage is displayed; a tenant may require a minimum completeness threshold before a deal advances beyond Discovery Call.

FR-7.3 — Lead scoring. Acceptance criteria: score combines fit attributes (industry, size, practice match) with engagement signals (activity recency, frequency, contact seniority) into a 0 to 100 value with visible contributing factors; scoring weights are admin-configurable; scores are recomputed on relevant writes.

FR-7.4 — Routing and response SLA. Acceptance criteria: rules assign new leads by practice line, territory, named account or round robin among available owners; an unassigned lead escalates after a configurable interval; time to first engagement is measured against a tenant SLA and reported.

FR-7.5 — Source attribution through to revenue. Acceptance criteria: the existing lead source field becomes a managed picklist with optional campaign tagging; Analytics reports lead volume, conversion rate and closed-won revenue by source and campaign.

EPIC 8 — Automation and Workflow (resolves D14 · Priority P2)

FR-8.1 — Rules engine. Acceptance criteria: a Tenant Admin may define rules of the form when trigger, if condition, then action; triggers include deal created, stage changed, value changed above threshold, activity logged, no activity for N days, task overdue, close date passed and deal escalated; actions include create task, assign owner, send notification, escalate, set field value and add to a review queue; rules are testable in a dry-run mode showing which existing records would match, are versioned, and every firing is logged and traceable from the affected record.

FR-8.2 — Stagnation and at-risk automation. Acceptance criteria: default shipped rules create a follow-up task when a deal has no client-facing activity for 14 days, escalate to Team Lead at 30 days and to Director at 45 days, and flag any deal whose close date has passed while still open; thresholds are tenant-configurable and align with the existing stage-age bands.

FR-8.3 — Stage-gate validation. Acceptance criteria: a Tenant Admin may define required fields and required artefacts per stage per practice line, for example a named contact and an attached document before Proposal Sent, a negotiated value before Negotiation, and a signed document reference before Project Start; advancement is blocked with a clear explanation of what is missing; Directors may override a gate with a mandatory reason, which is audited.

EPIC 9 — Collaboration and Notifications (resolves D24, extends C20 · Priority P2)

FR-9.1 — Human-event notifications. Acceptance criteria: the notification model is extended beyond system events to cover task assigned to you, task reassigned, task overdue, mention of you, comment on your deal, deal escalated to you, deal reassigned to you, and quota milestone; each type is independently controllable in user preferences, which today offer only three coarse toggles; the notification centre gains grouping by type and a filter for unread.

FR-9.2 — Two-way review thread. Acceptance criteria: Business Director Comments becomes a threaded conversation in which a BDM may reply, rather than a one-directional review note; unresolved comments are counted on the deal header; a comment may be marked resolved by the author or a Director.

FR-9.3 — Daily and weekly digests. Acceptance criteria: the existing weekly digest preference is honoured with real content — deals gone quiet, tasks due and overdue, deals awaiting review, stage movements and attainment pace — scoped to the recipient's role.

EPIC 10 — Documents, Templates and Content (resolves D15, D16 · Priority P2)

FR-10.1 — Deal-linked documents with versioning. Acceptance criteria: documents may be attached to a deal, an account, a contact or an activity, in addition to the current practice-line repository; uploading a file with an existing name against the same record creates a new version rather than a duplicate, with version history, uploader and timestamp; the latest version is served by default and prior versions remain retrievable; document type is classified as brief, proposal, contract, minutes or other.

FR-10.2 — Email and message templates. Acceptance criteria: templates support merge fields for contact, account, deal and user values; templates are shareable at practice or tenant level and usage is reported.

FR-10.3 — Outreach sequences. Acceptance criteria: a sequence defines an ordered set of steps with day offsets, each step being an email, call task or note; enrolling a contact schedules the steps as tasks and emails; replies detected through the email connector automatically pause the sequence; step-level open, reply and meeting-booked rates are reported.

FR-10.4 — Proposal generation and signature. Acceptance criteria: a proposal may be generated from a template populated with deal, account and contact data and exported as PDF; an integration point supports an external e-signature provider; signature status is written back to the deal and logged as an activity.

EPIC 11 — Data Quality, Governance and Trust (resolves D10, D17, D18, D21 · Priority P2)

FR-11.1 — In-app audit log viewer. Acceptance criteria: Tenant Admins gain a searchable, filterable audit view covering record create, update, delete and retract, permission and role changes, configuration changes, login events and rule firings, filterable by actor, entity, action type and date range, with CSV export retained; each record page exposes its own change history to Directors and above.

FR-11.2 — Duplicate detection and merge. Acceptance criteria: fuzzy matching warns on creation of a likely duplicate account, contact or deal; an admin merge tool consolidates records, preserving all activities, tasks, documents and deal links, with the merge itself audited and reversible for 30 days.

FR-11.3 — Soft delete and recycle bin. Acceptance criteria: no user-facing action performs a hard delete; deleted records enter a recycle bin retained for 30 days, consistent with the existing backup retention policy, from which a Tenant Admin may restore; permanent purge remains a platform super-admin action, consistent with the current restore-request model.

FR-11.4 — Multi-currency. Acceptance criteria: deals may be denominated in any enabled currency; the tenant defines a reporting currency; FX rates are stored with an effective date and applied at deal close for actuals and at current rate for open pipeline; all analytics display the reporting currency with the original denomination available on the record; the custom currency field currently used as a workaround is retired.

FR-11.5 — Required-field policy engine. Acceptance criteria: beyond stage gates, a Tenant Admin may mark any standard or custom field as required at creation or at a named stage, with the policy surfaced to users as inline validation rather than a save-time failure.

EPIC 12 — Platform, API and Mobile (resolves D19, D20, D22 · Priority P2 to P3)

FR-12.1 — Public REST API. Acceptance criteria: authenticated, tenant-scoped, rate-limited CRUD endpoints for accounts, contacts, leads, deals, activities and tasks, honouring the same role and practice-line permissions as the interface; versioned with a documented deprecation policy; API keys are issued and revocable by Tenant Admin and every call is attributable in the audit log.

FR-12.2 — Outbound webhooks. Acceptance criteria: subscribable events for deal created, stage changed, deal won, deal lost, task assigned and task overdue, with signed payloads, retry with exponential backoff, and a delivery log.

FR-12.3 — Chat and ERP integration. Acceptance criteria: Slack or Microsoft Teams channel notifications for stage changes, wins, escalations and overdue tasks; a documented export or integration path to the finance system for closed-won deals to support invoicing hand-off.

FR-12.4 — Responsive progressive web app. Acceptance criteria: every screen is usable at 375 pixels width; logging an activity, completing a task and viewing My Work are achievable in three taps or fewer from launch; the app is installable and caches the user's deals and tasks for read access when offline, queueing activity and task writes made offline for synchronisation on reconnect.

FR-12.5 — Saved views and scheduled reports. Acceptance criteria: users may save filter combinations as named views, private or shared with their team; Directors and Executives may subscribe to a scheduled report delivered on a defined cadence; Analytics gains CSV and PDF export.

EPIC 13 — AI Assistance (resolves D23 · Priority P3)

FR-13.1 — Deal risk scoring. Acceptance criteria: a risk score derived from engagement recency and frequency, stage velocity against practice benchmarks, single-threading, stage regression, overdue tasks and close-date slippage, with the contributing factors always shown so the score is explainable and never a black box; the existing static "Pipeline Health Check" panel is replaced by this.

FR-13.2 — Next-best-action recommendation. Acceptance criteria: recommendations are generated from patterns in historically won deals within the same practice line, presented as suggestions the user may accept as a task or dismiss, with dismissal feedback captured.

FR-13.3 — Engagement summarisation. Acceptance criteria: on demand, the system summarises a deal's engagement history into a briefing note for a Director or an incoming owner; the summary is always labelled as generated, is never stored as an activity entry, and cites the entries it draws from.

FR-13.4 — Natural-language reporting. Acceptance criteria: a question-and-answer interface over the tenant's own pipeline data, strictly honouring the requesting user's role and practice-line scope, returning both a narrative answer and the underlying figures.

7.2 Modifications to the deals table

Add: account_id (foreign key, replacing free-text client organisation), last_engaged_at, last_engaged_activity_id, engagement_count, next_action_task_id, next_action_due_date, forecast_category, forecast_category_override_by, forecast_category_override_reason, currency_code, risk_score, risk_factors JSON, and created_by backfilled to eliminate the "Unknown Author" state. Enforce not-null on owner_id and expected_close_date for any deal in an active status.

7.3 Migration requirements

Migrations must be reversible and must not lose data. Specifically: existing Internal Strategy Notes values convert to note-type activities dated to deal creation and attributed to the deal author; existing stage date fields generate reconstructed stage events flagged as such and excluded from cycle-time statistics; free-text client organisation values resolve into accounts with a fuzzy-match review queue for ambiguous cases; existing single contact person and email become the primary contact on the deal; the demo custom fields that duplicate incoming standard functionality (implementation notes, forecast uplift, decision deadline, priority tier) are reviewed with Sales Operations, and any that map to a new standard field are migrated and retired to free custom-field quota; unassigned and undated active deals are routed to an admin remediation queue rather than being silently defaulted.

8. Non-Functional Requirements

NFR-1 Security and tenancy. Row-level security on every new table keyed to tenant and practice-line entitlement. No client-side authorisation decisions; every read and write is authorised server-side. Email OAuth tokens encrypted at rest and never returned to the browser. Executive read-only enforcement must be verified server-side for all new write endpoints, since the current interface-level enforcement must not become the only control.

NFR-2 Permission regression testing. Because the root cause of the highest-severity finding is a permission-matrix error, a machine-readable permission matrix must be maintained as the single source of truth, with automated tests asserting, for every role and every action, both the ability and the inability. No release may ship with an untested cell in that matrix.

NFR-3 Auditability and immutability. Activities, stage events and audit entries are append-only. Corrections happen through revisions and retractions, never through overwriting or deletion.

NFR-4 Time correctness. All timestamps stored in UTC and rendered in the user's configured timezone, defaulting to West Central Africa. The distinction between engagement date and record creation timestamp must be preserved everywhere, including exports and the API.

NFR-5 Performance. Deal detail with a hundred activities renders within 1.5 seconds at the ninety-fifth percentile. My Work loads within one second for a user holding two hundred open tasks. Analytics queries over a hundred thousand deal-stage events return within three seconds. Derived fields such as last_engaged_at and next_action are updated transactionally on write, not by nightly batch.

NFR-6 Reliability. Automation rule firings and notification deliveries are idempotent and retried with backoff. No rule may fire twice for the same trigger instance. Email synchronisation failures degrade gracefully and never block manual logging.

NFR-7 Accessibility and usability. WCAG 2.1 AA for all new interfaces. Logging an activity must take no more than three interactions from the deal page. The keyboard path for log-activity and create-task must be complete.

NFR-8 Mobile. All new interfaces responsive from 375 pixels upward, with the offline write queue specified in FR-12.4.

NFR-9 Data retention and privacy. Email body storage is off by default and configurable at tenant level. Users may exclude individual threads. Contact and stakeholder data handling must align with applicable data protection law in each operating jurisdiction, and the tenant must be able to export or delete an individual's personal data on request. Personal data must not be transmitted through URL parameters.

NFR-10 Observability. Structured logging on every automation firing, sync job and permission denial. Product analytics instrumented against the Section 5.3 success metrics so adoption is measurable from day one rather than reconstructed later.

9.1 Dependency notes

Epic 2 depends on Epic 1, because a task without an engagement history has no context. Epic 4 analytics rebuild depends on FR-4.1 having accumulated real event data, so allow at least one full sales cycle between shipping the event log and trusting the derived metrics. Epic 5 depends on Epic 3, because automatic email logging requires contact records to match against. Epic 6 quota reporting depends on FR-6.1 and FR-6.5 data hygiene being enforced first. Epic 13 depends on twelve or more months of accumulated activity and outcome data; attempting it earlier will produce a model trained on emptiness.

9.2 Change management, which is not optional

The most likely cause of failure for this programme is not engineering. It is that activity logging is perceived by sellers as surveillance and administrative burden. Three mitigations should be treated as requirements rather than suggestions. First, ship Epic 5 email and calendar sync as early as dependencies allow, so that the majority of engagement is captured automatically rather than typed. Second, make leadership consume the activity data visibly in pipeline reviews within the first fortnight, so that logging has an obvious purpose. Third, hold the interaction cost genuinely low, which is the reason NFR-7 sets a three-interaction ceiling.

11. Open Questions for the Product Owner

The following require business decisions before the relevant epic can be estimated.

Should activity logging be mandatory before stage advancement, or encouraged through visibility alone? The answer determines whether FR-8.3 stage gates include an engagement condition.

Is the fiscal period a calendar quarter, and does it align across all seven practice lines? Quotas, forecast snapshots and attainment reporting all depend on this.

Should a BDM be able to see deals belonging to peers within the same practice line, or only their own? Current behaviour is ambiguous between the Business Lines and Pipeline Deals views, and the answer shapes the FR-2.2 assignee scope.

Who owns an account when multiple practice lines sell into the same client, which the current data already exhibits? This determines whether account ownership is singular, or a per-practice relationship.

What is the definition of a qualified opportunity for the sales-velocity calculation — entry to Discovery Call, or a qualification completeness threshold?

Should Executive read-only extend to reading engagement narratives, or only aggregate metrics? This is a trust and culture decision as much as a permissions one.

Is there an existing finance or ERP system that closed-won deals must hand off to, and does it constrain the FR-12.3 integration design?
